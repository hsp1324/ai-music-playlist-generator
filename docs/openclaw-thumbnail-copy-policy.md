# Audience-First Cover and Thumbnail Copy

Standing human direction, 2026-10-02 (Asia/Seoul). Read before planning any new image package on **every channel**, including custom/manual channels when requested.

## Apply prospectively; preserve existing work

- Do not delete, replace, regenerate, reopen, or repackage songs, images, releases, renders, or published videos already made/planned before this direction. Continue those releases with their existing approved assets and brief.
- Apply this policy to new release briefs and newly commissioned image packages from this direction onward. It is not a bulk thumbnail migration. Retain assets and existing job/order/schedule state.
- This policy controls image **wording**, not a new mandatory font or channel identity. It supersedes older requirements to stamp bare genre labels on new images. Existing channel-specific text-free defaults remain text-free.
- Read [Suno v6 policy](openclaw-suno-v6-policy.md) separately for new music and permitted reuse.

## The copy decision

Choose the listener's reason to press play, not a database category. `GAME OST`, `RPG OST`, `FILM SCORE`, `J-POP`, `SOLO PIANO`, and `TECH HOUSE` alone are not new primary image headlines. Accurate genre words remain useful in the YouTube title, description, playlist bucket, or a short phrase with an actual listening promise.

For example, replace bare `GAME OST` with `기분 좋은 게임 OST 모음`, `모험을 떠나고 싶은 날`, or `Feel-Good Game Music` **only when the music supports that mood**. A dramatic score might instead promise `Your Next Great Adventure`; do not label cute town music as epic battle music.

- One idea: a concrete feeling, situation, or benefit. Write naturally, not as an all-caps keyword stack or a fictional track title nobody recognizes.
- Prefer roughly 2–6 Korean words or 3–7 English words and no more than two short lines; these are composition heuristics, not rigid character limits. Native Japanese/Spanish phrasing should read naturally at phone size.
- Text is optional. A compelling text-free image is valid, especially for existing text-free channel identities. Do not put new lettering onto every channel just to show this policy was applied.
- A playback cover may remain clean while its same-scene thumbnail carries the hook. If the cover/first frame has text, keep it stable in any provider clip; do not add new text during motion.
- Keep lettering directly on the artwork with transparent background, safe margins, and readable local contrast. Existing no-box/no-badge/no-channel-brand/no-duration/no-waveform rules remain in force. Do not force every hook into the same upper-left label template.
- Match language to the channel's established audience. The user's Korean game-music example is welcome on Korean-facing game releases; do not translate all channels into Korean. Do not copy competitors' brands, artwork, protected characters, or franchises.
- Check at least the latest five channel packages when available. Vary the **listener promise**, not just one adjective; do not stamp “기분 좋은” or “Night Drive” on everything.

## New planning and review contract

Record in the production plan (these are planning fields, not newly added API flags):

- `listener_promise`: one sentence the actual music fulfils.
- `copy_language`, `cover_text`, `thumbnail_text` (empty string explicitly means text-free).
- Two candidate hooks, the selected hook, and why it is more inviting than a bare genre label; for text-free packages, explain the image's emotion instead.
- `recent_copy_checked`: avoid repeating the last five hooks/scenes when available.

Before approval, view the final image at mobile scale. Confirm that text is actually legible, the main subject remains clear, and the first tracks support the promise. Reject a newly generated bare-tag headline or unexpected lettering, not an existing approved legacy asset. Do not claim a CTR improvement without measured evidence.

## Research checked 2026-10-02

This is a bounded sample of current public channel pages/thumbnails, **not** a universal trend, a performance ranking, or an A/B result. Mood-led titles and strong text-free imagery coexist:

- essential; combines everyday emotional/situational titles with scene-led photos. Its [autumn-night playlist](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gwswm8OyMCs) and [clear-sky playlist](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VOPCgZWjSaw) sell a listening moment; the observed images mainly use its brand, not a paragraph of mood copy. We adopt the listener-promise principle, **not** its branding.
- Chillhop pairs season/editorial framing with coherent illustrated scenes: [Best of Essentials Fall](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M5hZAL_hW7c). Its [Sleepy Fish release](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xO8bQKdpfX8) also demonstrates a strong text-free image.
- GameChops' [Chocobo & Chill 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vSKitLixyMg) and [Zelda & Sleep](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGMqgzx5irY) connect game identity to a mood/activity instead of only “RPG OST.” These are observations, not permission to use those franchises or copy their visual assets.
- MrSuicideSheep's [Emotions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7yPt4jhP2bA) and [Memories](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m_EbHXhNKtA) show emotion-led photography/illustration without mandatory headline text.

[YouTube's official thumbnail/title guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en) recommends simple, readable, audience-relevant packaging and accurate titles. Our specific hook examples are an editorial decision implementing the human's preference, not an official YouTube formula.

Evaluate new packages using the channel's own impressions, Home/Suggested CTR, and viewing outcomes; public view counts do not reveal CTR. When eligible, a human can use [YouTube's title/thumbnail A/B testing](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en), which judges watch-time outcomes rather than CTR alone. No test has been run here. Automation must not open YouTube Studio or start OAuth to do this.
